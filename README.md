The client was "The Gaming Room" and they wanted to expand their Android game, Draw it or Lose it, so players could use it on different platforms. 
The software also needed to support multiple users, teams, and games while keeping everything organized and secure.
I think I did a good job comparing the different operating systems and explaining which choices would work best for the client.
I also looked at important areas like storage, memory, security, and how the game could work across different devices.
Working through the design document helped me organize my ideas before getting into the code.
It also made me think more about what the client actually needed, how the classes would connect, and what type of system would be best for the software.
Having everything planned out first made the whole development process easier for me to understand.

If I could go back and revise one part of my work, I would probably add more detail to some of my platform comparisons.
Instead of only explaining the advantages and disadvantages, I would give more information about why one platform might be a better choice than another for The Gaming Room.
I think that would make my recommendation clearer and help the client understand the reasoning behind my choices.
When I was designing the software, I focused on what The Gaming Room needed the system to do, such as working on multiple platforms, supporting different players and teams, and keeping information secure
Thinking about those needs helped guide the decisions I made throughout the design.

I approached the project by breaking everything down into smaller sections instead of trying to look at the whole thing at once.
I compared the operating systems, reviewed the UML diagram, and looked at storage, memory, networking, and security separately.
That made the project easier for me to understand and helped me stay organized.
I also learned that paying attention to the user’s needs is important because the software should actually solve the problem they are having and work the way they expect it to.
In the future, I would use a similar approach when designing another software application.
I would still break the requirements into smaller parts, compare different options, and make sure I understand what the client needs before making final design decisions.
